\begin{abstract}
Activation steering can change language-model behavior, but global
residual-stream shifts may suppress useful and harmful behavior together. For
reasoning confidence, the standard additive edit removes overconfident errors
only by collapsing justified confidence, so apparent calibration gains can
reflect indiscriminate hedging rather than better control. We introduce
Projection-Transport Steering, which treats steering as a projection-dependent
transport problem and applies an action only when a separate trace-level
detector fires. This separates the decision to intervene from the intervention
itself, allowing abnormal activation tails to move while typical states remain
largely untouched. Across five resampled MMLU subsets, gated ablation reaches
selectivity $+0.266$ with no detectable accuracy change; additive steering
provides essentially no selectivity and lowers accuracy. In a held-out
OLMo-2/TruthfulQA evaluation, the detector-axis action improves calibrated
truthfulness and correct-answer selection over a norm-matched action, with all
seven predeclared construction controls passing. Formal results characterize
the minimum projection-local intervention cost and detector-score separability
under stated assumptions. These findings show that selective behavior control
depends less on pushing activations harder than on separating detection from
action. Steering systems should first determine when intervention is warranted,
then use an input-dependent, projection-local edit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Inference-time activation steering promises behavior control without retraining:
add a direction to the residual stream and the model refuses less, hedges more,
or changes style. The workhorse is contrastive activation addition
(CAA)~\cite{rimsky2024caa}: a fixed vector, a hand-tuned coefficient, applied to
every token. Yet applied to a concrete reasoning behavior; overconfidence
of \texttt{gemma-2-2b-it} on multiple-choice reasoning; CAA fails in a way
no coefficient tuning fixes: the dose that eliminates
\emph{overconfident-wrong} answers (\ocw{} $52\!\to\!0$ under a calibrated
readout) simultaneously eliminates \emph{confident-right} ones (\crok{}
$77\!\to\!1$) and costs $8.3$ accuracy points.\looseness=-1

This paper treats that failure as a mathematical object rather than an
engineering nuisance. Our contributions:

\begin{compactenum}
\item \textbf{A unifying framework with optimality theorems.} Every linear
steering operator is a scalar transfer function $g$ on the projection
$p=\langle h,\hat v\rangle$ (additive: $g(p){=}p{+}\alpha$; ablation: $g{=}0$;
CAST-style gating~\cite{lee2024cast}: $g(p){=}p{+}\alpha\mathbf{1}[p{>}\tau]$).
The optimal-transport map between projection laws is the unique
minimal-perturbation intervention; additive steering is optimal only for
pure location shifts (Thm.~\ref{thm:ot}, Cor.~\ref{cor:additive}).
\item \textbf{A quantified impossibility result and its repair.} Selectivity
of \emph{any} transfer function on an axis is capped by subpopulation
separability along it (Thm.~\ref{thm:gate}b); we measure token-level
$d=0.049$ between \ocw{} and \crok{} on the confidence axis; the additive
collapse is geometric necessity. The repair is conditional: an LDA gate on a
second, detection axis is Neyman--Pearson optimal, and gate$\times$transport
is jointly optimal in its class (Thm.~\ref{thm:joint}).
\item \textbf{Strong trade-offs at minimal action cost in the evaluated setting.} Across five
MMLU seeds our conditional operators reach selectivity $\approx+0.2$--$0.27$
(additive $\approx0$, prompting $+0.07$) at zero accuracy cost, dominate
faithful reimplementations of CAST, MiMiC, and Linear-AcT on the
selectivity--accuracy--compute frontier, and replicate at $4.5\times$ scale
(9B). Transfer to ARC/GSM8K needs no refitting. Every operator is $O(d)$
per token with 19~KB state (Prop.~\ref{prop:compute}: optimal order).\looseness=-1
\end{compactenum}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Linear steering.} ActAdd~\cite{turner2023actadd} and
CAA~\cite{rimsky2024caa} add fixed diff-in-means vectors;
ITI~\cite{li2023iti} shifts probe-selected heads; RepE~\cite{zou2023repe}
generalizes reading/control vectors; directional ablation removes one
direction everywhere~\cite{arditi2024refusal}; SAE clamping fixes a feature
value~\cite{templeton2024scaling}. All are special cases of our
transfer-function family, and all are input-independent edits.
AxBench~\cite{wu2025axbench} finds them weaker than prompting, so a new
method must justify itself on locality, dose control, and serving cost, which
we formalize. CAST~\cite{lee2024cast} adds conditional \emph{gating}, but its
action remains additive; SADI~\cite{wang2025sadi} and KL-adaptive
composition~\cite{scalena2024dynamic} adapt dose per input.\looseness=-1

\textbf{Distribution matching and optimality.} MiMiC~\cite{singh2024mimic}
derives the Gaussian-$\Wtwo$ affine map, applied unconditionally in the full
space; AcT~\cite{rodriguez2025act} fits per-neuron 1-D transport;
LinEAS~\cite{rodriguez2025lineas} learns affine maps under a distributional
loss. COAST~\cite{nguyen2026coast} proves \emph{pointwise} optimality of a
covariance-weighted projection; in our language, transport to a point mass
(their $\Sigma$-metric is the $M$ of Prop.~\ref{prop:metric}); A-LQR
\cite{nguyen2026alqr} proves LQR-optimality of a control input. Our
Theorem~\ref{thm:ot} is a different statement: the optimal map between
\emph{distributions} under a locality constraint, with the conditional
optimality and selectivity ceiling (Thms.~\ref{thm:gate}--\ref{thm:joint})
that no prior steering work states. PCHI~\cite{liu2026probe} trains
probe-conditioned head rescalings for the same behavior; its additive
baseline corrects $\approx0\%$, independently confirming
Cor.~\ref{cor:additive}. \cite{zhang2026wired} reports the dose--response
our per-sample dose exploits.

\section{Method: Projection-Transport Steering}
\label{sec:method}

Let $h\in\mathbb{R}^d$ be the residual-stream activation at a fixed decoder layer
and let $V\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}$ have orthonormal rows spanning a
\emph{behavioral subspace} (here $k\le2$: a confidence axis and a correctness
axis obtained by diff-in-means on the model's own labeled reasoning traces).
Write $q=Vh\in\mathbb{R}^k$, let $\mu$ be the law of $h$ under natural
generation, $\mu_V$ the induced law of $q$, and $\nu$ a target law on
$\mathbb{R}^k$.

\begin{definition}[admissible steering]
$T:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d$ is admissible for $(V,\nu)$ if
(i) $(I-V^\top V)\,T(h)=(I-V^\top V)\,h$ $\mu$-a.s.\ (\emph{locality}: the
orthogonal complement is untouched), and (ii) $(V\!\cdot T)_{\#}\mu=\nu$, i.e.\ the pushforward of $\mu$ equals the target law
(\emph{goal}: steered projections follow the target law). The cost is
$C(T)=\mathbb{E}_\mu\lVert T(h)-h\rVert^2$.
\end{definition}

The cost is the right regularizer: large interventions are what empirically
destroy generation (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}), and under a quadratic functional
metric $C$ bounds the KL drift of the next-token distribution
(Prop.~\ref{prop:metric}).

\begin{theorem}[steering is optimal transport of the projection]
\label{thm:ot}
$\min_T C(T) = \Wtwo^2(\mu_V,\nu)$ over admissible $T$, attained by
$T^*(h)=h+(S(Vh)-Vh)^\top V$ where $S$ is the ($\mu_V$-a.s.\ unique) optimal
Monge map from $\mu_V$ to $\nu$. Any optimal $T$ satisfies $V T(h)=S(Vh)$ a.s.;
in particular the optimal intervention is a deterministic function of the
projection alone; conditioning on $h_\perp$ cannot reduce the cost.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Locality collapses the cost to the projection; $(Vh,VT(h))$ couples
$(\mu_V,\nu)$, so $C(T)\ge\Wtwo^2$; Brenier's map attains it. Full proof in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

For $k=1$, $S=F_\nu^{-1}\!\circ F_{\mu_V}$ is the monotone quantile map,
implemented as a 41-knot piecewise-linear lookup. For Gaussian marginals the map
is affine (Knott--Smith~\cite{knott1984optimal}; Gelbrich~\cite{gelbrich1990formula}):
$S(q)=m_t+A(q-m_s)$ with
$A=\Sigma_s^{-1/2}\big(\Sigma_s^{1/2}\Sigma_t\Sigma_s^{1/2}\big)^{1/2}\Sigma_s^{-1/2}$.

\begin{corollary}[additive steering is forced to be global]
\label{cor:additive}
Additive steering $g(p)=p+\alpha$ is the optimal map iff $\nu=\mu_V\!*\!\delta_\alpha$
(pure shift). If the target compresses the spread ($\sigma_t<\sigma_s$; what
``hedge only abnormal confidence'' requires), the optimal slope is
$\sigma_t/\sigma_s<1$: the tail must move more than the bulk. A slope-1 map
matched to the tail over-transports the bulk by the same constant; the
observed \crok{} collapse. Moreover its selectivity between two subpopulations
with equal projection laws is identically zero.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[optimal gate; selectivity ceiling]
\label{thm:gate}
Let a gate fire iff a trace statistic $s>\tau$, with
$\mathrm{TPR}(\tau)=P(s>\tau\mid \ocw)$, $\mathrm{FPR}(\tau)=P(s>\tau\mid \crok)$.
(a) For any objective increasing in TPR and decreasing in FPR, the
likelihood-ratio test is optimal at every operating point (Neyman--Pearson); under
equal-covariance Gaussian class conditionals it reduces to the linear
discriminant $w=\Sigma^{-1}(\mu_{\ocw}-\mu_{\lnot\ocw})$.
(b) For any statistic with concavified ROC area $\mathrm{AUC}$,
$\max_\tau[\mathrm{TPR}-\mathrm{FPR}] \le 2\,\mathrm{AUC}-1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[joint optimality of gate $\times$ transport]
\label{thm:joint}
Among interventions that are local to $V$, identity off the gate event
$\Gamma=\{s>\tau\}$, and achieve target law $\nu_\Gamma$ on $\Gamma$, the
cost-minimal intervention applies Theorem~\ref{thm:ot}'s map for the conditional
law $(\mu_V\mid\Gamma)\to\nu_\Gamma$ on $\Gamma$; its cost is
$P(\Gamma)\,\Wtwo^2(\mu_V\!\mid\!\Gamma,\ \nu_\Gamma)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[metric covariance]
\label{prop:metric}
If cost is $(\Delta h)^\top M(\Delta h)$ with $M\succ0$ (e.g.\ a Fisher/Gauss--Newton
pullback, so cost approximates the induced next-token KL), the substitution
$y=M^{1/2}h$ reduces the problem to Theorem~\ref{thm:ot}; Euclidean PTS is
$M=I$. Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[support preservation]
The quantile map never leaves the target's empirical support; an additive
map violates support for every $\alpha\neq0$, consistent with the observed
generation collapse at $|\alpha|=1.5\lVert h\rVert$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[computational optimality]
\label{prop:compute}
Any algorithm implementing a non-degenerate intervention local to a subspace
spanned by a dense direction must read all $d$ coordinates of $h$, i.e.\ costs
$\Omega(d)$ per token. PTS costs $\Theta(kd+\log K)$; matching the lower
bound for constant $k,K$; versus $\Theta(d^2)$ for a full-space affine map
(MiMiC), $\Theta(d\,d_{\mathrm{sae}})$ for SAE steering, and a full extra
forward pass for KL-adaptive dosing. Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proposition}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp}\label{sec:data}

\textbf{Models, datasets, and splits.} The main experiments use \texttt{gemma-2-2b-it}~\cite{gemma2024}; the
central comparison is repeated on \texttt{gemma-2-9b-it} at $4.5\times$
scale (Sec.~\ref{ssec:ninetb}).\footnote{2B weights were served from the
\texttt{unsloth/gemma-2-2b-it} mirror; we verified all 288 tensors are
bit-identical to the official \texttt{google/gemma-2-2b-it} release.}
Both models reason inside \texttt{<think>...</think>} and answer
\verb|\boxed{letter}|; decoding is greedy, so evaluation ``seeds''
resample \emph{questions}, not decoding noise. Activations are read and
modified at layer 14/26 (2B) and 24/42 (9B). Benchmarks:
MMLU~\cite{hendrycks2021mmlu} (fitting and primary evaluation),
ARC-Challenge~\cite{clark2018arc}, and GSM8K~\cite{cobbe2021gsm8k} as
4-option MCQ with seeded distractors (GSM8K-MCQ, Appendix~\ref{app:gsm});
$n{=}300$ questions each, disjoint from all fitting data. Directions,
maps, and gate parameters are fit \emph{once} per scale on a 400-question
MMLU extraction split ($\sim$50k trace tokens) and reused everywhere:
cross-benchmark results are transfer, not refits. The main comparison
uses five resampled MMLU subsets; the 9B replication three.

\textbf{Behavioral states and confidence readouts.} Every answer gets one of four labels; \emph{overconfident-wrong} (\ocw),
\emph{nonconfident-wrong}, \emph{nonconfident-right},
\emph{confident-right} (\crok); under two independent readouts: the
\emph{letter-logit} readout M2 (softmax of the letter logits at the
trace's own answer position) and the \emph{yes-probability} readout M4
(normalized $P(\mathrm{YES})$ over per-option yes/no reformulations). On
identical questions they disagree sharply (ECE $0.39$ vs $0.10$;
Appendix~\ref{sec:measure}): M2 is strongly overconfident, M4 much better
calibrated. M4 is therefore the primary readout; agreement under M2 and
the M3 self-report is a robustness requirement. Full grids:
Appendix~\ref{app:fulltables}.\looseness=-1

\textbf{Directions and the trace-level gate.} On the extraction split we fit a \emph{confidence} axis $\hat v$ (the
steering axis) and a \emph{detection} axis $\hat u$, both diff-in-means,
orthonormalized into $V=(\hat v,\hat u)$. A completed trace of length $T$
is summarized by its mean projections
$z=\big(\tfrac1T\sum_t\langle h_t,\hat v\rangle,\
\tfrac1T\sum_t\langle h_t,\hat u\rangle\big)$; an LDA head gives the gate
score $s(z)=w^\top z+b$, firing iff $s(z)>\tau$. Gated conditions are
\emph{two-pass}: generate unsteered; if the gate is silent, keep that
output; else regenerate with the intervention active during reasoning.
The gate sees only the model's own activations; no gold labels at
inference.\looseness=-1

\textbf{Steering methods.} All operators act through $p_t=\langle h_t,\hat v\rangle$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:method}). \emph{Additive}: $h_t'=h_t+\alpha\hat v$,
$\alpha=-0.75\lVert h\rVert$ (the dose that eliminates \ocw{} under the
calibrated readout). \emph{Ablation}: $h_t'=h_t-p_t\hat v$.
\emph{Clamp}: $p_t'=\min(p_t,c)$ at a source quantile (median =
representative condition). \emph{Quantile/Gaussian/BW transport}:
the monotone quantile or affine Gaussian/Bures--Wasserstein map onto a
target law fitted on the extraction split. \emph{Conditional variants}:
the trace gate composed with each action (CAST-style gate$+$additive,
gated clamp, gated ablation, gated OT, LDA-gated subspace BW); the gate
controls \emph{exposure}, the action \emph{severity}, especially on false
positives. \emph{Published baselines}: prompting, additive
CAA~\cite{rimsky2024caa}, ablation~\cite{arditi2024refusal},
CAST-style~\cite{lee2024cast}, MiMiC~\cite{singh2024mimic}, Linear-AcT
per-neuron transport~\cite{rodriguez2025act}; all reimplemented and run
on identical records, decoding, readouts, and metrics.
\emph{Think-only variants} disable steering before the answer tokens.

\textbf{Metrics and statistical protocol.} Each metric answers one question about the intervention.

\emph{Accuracy}: did it hurt task performance? We report the change
$\Delta$acc against the unsteered model.

\emph{ECE} (expected calibration error): can the confidence numbers be
trusted? The mean absolute gap between average stated confidence and
actual accuracy over 10 confidence bins (lower is better; $0$ =
confidence matches reality).

\emph{State histogram}: where did the answers move? The counts of the
four behavioral states before and after intervention.\looseness=-1

\emph{Surgical metrics}: did it hit only its target? Pairing answers by
question, $\mathrm{ocw\_rm}=P(\text{left \ocw}\mid\text{was \ocw})$ is
the fraction of the harmful state removed,
$\mathrm{cr\_keep}=P(\text{stayed \crok}\mid\text{was \crok})$ the
fraction of the healthy state preserved, and selectivity
$\mathrm{Sel}=\mathrm{ocw\_rm}-(1-\mathrm{cr\_keep})$ combines them
($+1$ = perfect scalpel; $0$ = indiscriminate).\looseness=-1

\emph{Selective prediction} (AURC/E-AURC): does confidence still rank
right answers above wrong ones? AURC is the area under the risk--coverage
curve (lower is better); monotone post-hoc recalibration leaves it
invariant, so only interventions that change answers or their ranking can
move it.

Single-subset results carry paired-bootstrap 95\% CIs; multi-subset
results are mean$\pm$std over independently resampled subsets.\looseness=-1

\textbf{Post-review validation protocol.} We froze a simplified detector-axis
action after a development-only Qwen3 component analysis, then evaluated it on
held-out TruthfulQA panels for Mistral-7B, OLMo-2-7B, and Granite-8B. Each
eligible architecture used the same 237-question composition (50 in-domain,
187 shifted) and was compared with a norm-matched spherical action using
10,000 paired BCa resamples; the three architecture comparisons used
Bonferroni-adjusted two-sided intervals. OLMo-2 subsequently faced seven
pre-frozen construction and specificity controls. A separate six-cell
Mistral/OLMo-2/Granite $\times$ BBQ/ETHICS observer screen kept fitting,
screening, and confirmatory units disjoint; only Mistral/ETHICS advanced to a
1,000-scenario confirmatory test, with intervals adjusted for the six planned
cells. Only observer-eligible cells were opened; the planned denominator remained six.

\textbf{The confidence axis does not separate justified from unjustified
confidence.} Figure~\ref{fig:cdf} tests the geometric premise of selective steering:
the \ocw{} and \crok{} projection CDFs nearly coincide ($d=0.049$) while
the confident/nonconfident pairs separate ($d\approx0.6$). The axis
encodes \emph{whether} the model is confident, not whether the confidence
is warranted, so no transfer function of this coordinate can be surgical
(Cor.~\ref{cor:additive}; Thm.~\ref{thm:gate}b,
$\mathrm{AUC}\approx.51$). The measurements agree: the selected dose
removes all 52 \ocw{} but takes \crok{} from 77 to 1; across five subsets
additive selectivity is $+0.003\pm0.006$, accuracy drops $7.8\pm1.5$
points, AURC worsens $0.410\to0.534$. Eliminating \ocw{} is not evidence
of calibration: the shift suppresses desirable and undesirable confidence
alike.\looseness=-1

\textbf{Projection-dependent actions reduce collateral damage.}
Median clamping; one scalar touching only the abnormal tail; reaches
selectivity $+.234$ on the representative subset at $+5.3$ accuracy
points; across five subsets accuracy is $+0.007\pm0.032$, i.e.\
\emph{preserved}, not improved (single-split accuracy gains should be
treated as noise until seed-replicated), while selectivity holds at
$+0.199\pm0.071$. Quantile transport to the non-\ocw{} target reaches
$+0.141\pm0.083$, likewise without the additive loss. The lesson is
shape, not strength: abnormal high-confidence activations must move more
than ordinary ones, which ablation, clamp, and transport achieve by
conditioning on the input projection.\looseness=-1

\textbf{Main result.} Across five MMLU subsets our gated ablation attains
selectivity $\mathbf{+0.266\pm0.112}$ (additive $+0.003\pm0.006$,
prompting $+0.066$) at no detectable accuracy cost ($+0.009\pm0.036$),
with the best ECE ($.093\pm.021$) and AURC ($.387\pm.045$), beating

\section{Limitations and Assumptions}

The optimal-transport results concern minimal activation displacement under a
specified subspace, target projection law, and locality constraint; they do not
establish that the chosen projection is sufficient for downstream behavior.
The Monge statement also requires the usual existence and regularity conditions
for a deterministic optimal map. The gate theorem bounds statistical
separability of its score, while behavioral utility additionally depends on the
action and model response. Its area bound uses the concavified ROC, not raw
empirical AUROC.

Empirically, the five main evaluation seeds are independently resampled question
subsets under deterministic decoding, not independent training runs. The
original positive evidence covers overconfidence on two Gemma scales and
zero-shot benchmark transfer; the held-out extension adds one confirmed
OLMo-2/TruthfulQA result. Other architecture/behavior cells did not satisfy all
predeclared gates, and the advancing ETHICS cell exposed label-stratum
heterogeneity (Appendix~\ref{app:postreview}). These boundaries preclude a
universal detector-axis claim. Finally, the reported $O(d)$ bound is an
operator-cost claim; end-to-end latency for the evaluated post-hoc gate includes
a second generation for flagged traces.

\section{Conclusion}

We turned an empirical steering failure into theorems and a method: the
additive edit is the wrong shape for tail-compression targets; optimal
transport along a behavioral subspace is the provably minimal
intervention; selectivity is constrained by detection as well as action
response; and the NP-gate $\times$ tail-local action gives the strongest joint
trade-off in our Gemma/MMLU comparison across five subsets and two model scales,
at no detectable accuracy cost and minimal per-token action compute. Held-out
validation on OLMo-2/TruthfulQA supports the detector-axis construction beyond
Gemma. Future work: better detection axes (at 9B
AUROC rises to $0.83$), Fisher-metric transport, further
behaviors.\looseness=-1

\section{Full proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:ot}.} By locality, $T(h)-h\in\mathrm{span}(V)$
$\mu$-a.s., hence $\lVert T(h)-h\rVert^2=\lVert VT(h)-Vh\rVert^2$ and
$C(T)=\mathbb{E}\lVert VT(h)-Vh\rVert^2$. Let $\gamma$ be the joint law of
$(Vh,\,VT(h))$; by the goal constraint $\gamma$ is a coupling of $(\mu_V,\nu)$
and $C(T)=\int\lVert y-x\rVert^2\,d\gamma\ge\Wtwo^2(\mu_V,\nu)$. Since
activations are continuous, $\mu_V$ is atomless with finite second moment, so by
Brenier's theorem the optimal coupling is unique and induced by a map
$S=\nabla\varphi$, $\varphi$ convex; $T^*(h)=h+(S(Vh)-Vh)^\top V$ is admissible
and attains the bound. For any optimal $T$, the induced coupling must equal the
unique optimal one, i.e.\ $VT(h)=S(Vh)$ a.s. Since $S$ depends only on $Vh$, the
optimal intervention is $\sigma(Vh)$-measurable: access to $h_\perp$ is useless.
\qed

\textbf{Corollary~\ref{cor:additive}.} If $\nu=\mu_V*\delta_\alpha$ the monotone
map is $x\mapsto x+\alpha$. Conversely if the optimal map is $x+\alpha$ then
$\nu$ is the shift of $\mu_V$. For Gaussians with $\sigma_t\neq\sigma_s$, after
the best shift the remaining distance is
$\Wtwo^2(\mu_V*\delta_\alpha,\nu)=(\sigma_s-\sigma_t)^2>0$. Selectivity: a map
$g$ acts identically on two subpopulations whose laws of $p$ coincide; the
paired state changes it induces then differ only through the (equal)
distributions, giving equal marginal change rates, i.e.\ zero selectivity in
expectation. \qed

\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:gate}.} (a) is the Neyman--Pearson lemma applied to the
class-conditional laws of $s$; monotone-likelihood-ratio families reduce the LR
test to thresholding, and for $\mathcal{N}(\mu_B,\Sigma)$ vs
$\mathcal{N}(\mu_G,\Sigma)$ the log-LR is affine in $s$ with direction
$\Sigma^{-1}(\mu_B-\mu_G)$ (LDA). (b) The concavified ROC contains the segment
path $(0,0)\to(\mathrm{FPR}^*,\mathrm{TPR}^*)\to(1,1)$ through the
Youden-optimal point; its area is
$\tfrac12(1+\mathrm{TPR}^*-\mathrm{FPR}^*)=\tfrac{1+J}{2}\le\mathrm{AUC}$. \qed

\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:joint}.} Decompose
$C(T)=P(\Gamma)\mathbb{E}[\lVert\Delta\rVert^2\mid\Gamma]+P(\Gamma^c)\mathbb{E}[\lVert\Delta\rVert^2\mid\Gamma^c]$.
Identity off $\Gamma$ zeroes the second term and is forced by the constraint;
the first term with the conditional goal constraint is exactly the
Theorem~\ref{thm:ot} problem for $\mu(\cdot\mid\Gamma)$. The constraints and
objective separate over the partition $\{\Gamma,\Gamma^c\}$, so the piecewise
optimum is global. \qed

\textbf{Proposition~\ref{prop:metric}.} With $y=M^{1/2}h$,
$\lVert\Delta h\rVert_M^2=\lVert\Delta y\rVert^2$; locality w.r.t.\ $V$ becomes
locality w.r.t.\ $M^{1/2}V$ (a linear subspace), and pushforward constraints
transform covariantly under the isomorphism. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:ot} in
$y$-space and map back. \qed

\textbf{Proposition~\ref{prop:compute}.} \emph{Lower bound:} let $\hat v_j$ be
dense ($(\hat v_j)_i\neq0$ for all $i$) and $g$ non-constant in $p_j$. If an
algorithm leaves coordinate $i$ unread, an adversary perturbs $h_i$; this
changes $p_j=\langle h,\hat v_j\rangle$ and hence, on a set of positive
measure, the correct output $T(h)$, while the algorithm's output is unchanged;
contradiction. Learned diff-in-means directions are dense almost surely.
\emph{Upper bound:} PTS computes $p=Vh$ ($kd$ multiply-adds), a monotone
$K$-knot lookup ($O(\log K)$ by binary search), and $h+(g(p)-p)^\top V$ ($kd$
more): $\Theta(kd+\log K)$ total. Memory $\Theta(kd+kK)$: reproducing a target
marginal at quantile resolution $1/K$ requires $\Omega(K)$ parameters, and
specifying the subspace requires $kd$. \qed

\section{GSM8K MCQ construction}
\label{app:gsm}
Each GSM8K test item with integer gold $g$ receives three deterministic
distractors drawn (seeded by item index) from
$\{g\pm1, g\pm2, g\pm10, 2g, \lfloor g/2\rfloor, \mathrm{round}(1.5g),
\mathrm{round}(0.8g), g\pm\mathrm{U}\{3..9\}\}$, deduplicated, shuffled with the
gold into four options. This converts free-form arithmetic into selection while
keeping the reasoning burden; we report transfer numbers on it and do not claim
GSM8K free-form results.

\section{The behavior across domains and scales (measurement study)}
\label{sec:measure}

\textbf{Overconfidence is model $\times$ instrument (2B).} Five confidence
readouts on identical MMLU questions ($n{=}1000\times3$ seeds) disagree
categorically: ECE ranges from $.391\,[.374,.409]$ (letter-logit M2, the
literature default) through $.249$/$.177$ (self-report/answer-distribution)
to $.104\,[.086,.120]$ (per-option yes/no M4); disjoint CIs; the
instruments even disagree on the answer (all five agree on $28.9\%$ of
questions). Hence our protocol requires every steering claim to hold under
multiple readouts. \textbf{Domains:} on MMLU the model is overconfident
(52/300 \ocw{}), on GSM8K-MC and GPQA \emph{under}confident (6 and 27
\ocw{}); the failure mode is domain-dependent, and the input-dependent
operators inherit exactly this dependence (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}).

\textbf{Scale replication (9B).} The full pipeline was replicated on
\texttt{gemma-2-9b-it} (42 layers, $d{=}3584$, layer 24) over the same
domains. Method-dependence survives scale (MMLU ECE $.126$--$.266$, GPQA
$.177$--$.499$, same instrument ranking); scale \emph{polarizes} rather than
cures (GSM8K nearly solved and calibrated, gap $-0.015$; GPQA confidence
grows faster than knowledge, M2 conf $.86$ at acc $.36$); the confidence
cone replicates (cosines $0.63$--$0.83$); confidence becomes \emph{more}
linearly readable (detector AUROC to $0.83$ vs $0.62$--$0.67$ at 2B), so the
Thm.~\ref{thm:gate}b ceiling rises with scale; and steering remains
one-directional, as Remark~1 predicts.

\begin{abstract}
Аддитивный стиринг активаций; стандартный инструмент управления поведением
языковых моделей; в применении к сверхуверенности рассуждений действует как
глобальная ручка хеджирования: доза, устраняющая все уверенно-неверные ответы,
уничтожает и 98{,}7\% уверенно-верных. Мы объясняем и устраняем этот дефект.
Каждое линейное вмешательство представляется скалярной функцией переноса на
проекции активации; доказано, что карта оптимального транспорта между
распределениями проекций; единственное вмешательство минимальной нормы,
достигающее целевого поведенческого распределения, а аддитивный стиринг;
её вырожденный случай чистого сдвига. Доказан результат о невозможности:
селективность любого одномерного метода ограничена разделимостью
подпопуляций вдоль оси (измеренное $d=0{,}049$ делает аддитивный метод
принципиально нехирургичным). Комбинация оптимального по Нейману--Пирсону
гейта на оси детекции с транспортной картой даёт условный метод, оптимальный
в каждом факторе. Эмпирически простейший вариант (клэмп проекции) одновременно
снижает долю уверенно-неверных ответов на 23\%, сохраняет 79--90\%
уверенно-верных и статистически значимо повышает точность на MMLU при цене
в одно скалярное произведение на токен.
\end{abstract}
