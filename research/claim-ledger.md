## Claim policy

- A theorem claim must point to assumptions and a proof object.
- An implementation claim must point to a versioned test or instrumented receipt.
- An empirical claim must point to raw paired outputs, a frozen manifest, and an analysis hash.
- Absence of a significant degradation is not evidence of equivalence or non-inferiority.
- A detector score is not a steering score; a behavior score is not automatically a valid construct measure.
- Predictive accuracy is not decision quality; an observer is evaluated under the action set and loss it will control.
- Successful activation control is not evidence of a causal abstraction without interchange-intervention tests.
- A direction is defined by its construction and downstream function; it is not called canonical without an identification result.
- Static separability, cosine, or probe accuracy cannot substitute for paired action-value evidence.
- A claim is narrowed when evidence is narrow. It is never broadened by averaging heterogeneous tasks.
